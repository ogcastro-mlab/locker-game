The Locker Problem

An interactive exploration of factors, multiples, and square numbers through Mr. Wolf's 20-locker hallway challenge.

1

20 Lockers, All Closed

A school hallway contains 20 lockers numbered 1 to 20. Initially, every locker door is completely CLOSED.

2

20 Students Walk Down

20 students enter the hall one by one. Student 1 goes first, followed by Student 2, Student 3, all the way to Student 20.

3

The Multiples Rule

Student n toggles every n-th locker. (If open, they close it; if closed, they open it).

Challenge: Which lockers stay OPEN after all 20 students pass? Let's test it!
Section 2 • Guided Walkthrough

How Student Passes Work (First 4 Students)

Student 1 1-th multiples

Opens every single locker (1, 2, 3, 4 ... 20).

Result: All 20 Lockers OPEN
Student 2 2-nd multiples

Closes every even locker (2, 4, 6, 8 ... 20).

Result: Evens CLOSED, Odds OPEN
Student 3 3-rd multiples

Toggles every 3rd locker (3, 6, 9, 12 ... 18).

Result: 3 opens/closes inverted
Student 4 4-th multiples

Toggles every 4th locker (4, 8, 12, 16, 20).

Notice Locker 4 re-opens!

Section 3 • Inquiry & Discussion

Classroom Discussion Questions

Question 1

Which lockers are still open at the end of the game?

Answer: Perfect Squares! (1, 4, 9, 16). They have an odd number of factors (odd touches), leaving them OPEN!
Click to reveal answer
Question 2

Which lockers were touched by only two students?

Answer: Prime Numbers! (2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19). Prime numbers have exactly 2 factors: 1 and themselves.
Click to reveal answer
Question 3

Which lockers were touched by only three students?

Answer: Squares of Primes! Lockers 4 (factors: 1, 2, 4) and 9 (factors: 1, 3, 9).
Click to reveal answer
Question 4

Which lockers were touched the most?

Answer: Lockers 12 & 18! Each touched 6 times.
• 12: (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12)
• 18: (1, 2, 3, 6, 9, 18)
